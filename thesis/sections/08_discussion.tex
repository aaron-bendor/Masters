\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Cross-tier comparison}
\label{sec:disc-cross-tier}

The core inverse-MC scoring machinery ports cleanly across all three
tiers: the inverter, FD-checked gradient, contrast diagnostic, and
feature plumbing operate unchanged on a synthetic $n=100$ chain, a SUMO
$n=2{,}405$ network, and a real-world $n=1{,}733$ Xuancheng network. What
differs by tier is the loader, the regime definition, and the
OD-control logic; the shared scoring core is selected by a single
\code{--dataset \{sumo,xuancheng\}} command-line interface (CLI) flag. This is a useful
generalisation result for an inverse-MC pipeline that the original paper
validated only on synthetic data and one real-world demonstration.

What does \emph{not} generalise as cleanly is the magnitude of the
detection signal. Tier~1 reproduces the paper's RMAE within
$\sim 0.05$ for $|\Xo| \ge 10$. Tier~2 reaches $\AUC = 0.708$ at $T=40$
on the controlled SUMO comparison, $\sim 69\%$ of the gap to the
full-observation empirical $1$-step reference of $0.801$, with per-row
chain Pearson $\sim +0.21$. Tier~3 on Xuancheng, with no ground-truth
sat-navigation label, gives chance-level fitted AUC on the
rush-vs-off-peak behavioural proxy. A stronger Labour-Day proxy creates
a significant fine-OD empirical signal ($\AUC_{\mathrm{emp}}=0.567$),
but multistart validation-selected fitting still falls to chance
($\AUC_{\mathrm{fit}}=0.502$). The drop
between Tier~2 and Tier~3 has two distinct contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item The Xuancheng ceiling itself ($\AUC_{\mathrm{emp}} \sim 0.57$) is
much lower than the SUMO ceiling ($\sim 0.69$), reflecting that the
behavioural rush-vs-off-peak proxy is a weaker contrast than the
SUMO labelled comparison.
\item The fitted-vs-reference gap is \emph{not} merely a scaled-down
version of the SUMO success. The pooled un-matched Xuancheng row gives
a superficially moderate gap-closure number because the reference itself
is weak, but once OD mix is controlled the fitted detector sits at chance
across every tested $K$ while the full-observation reference remains
weakly above chance at finer granularities. Tier~3 is therefore a
genuine negative result for the current $1$-step partial-observation
filter under this behavioural proxy, not just ``less signal to recover''.
\item The Labour-Day stress test rules out the strongest alternative
reading: that a more intuitive real-world contrast would rescue the
detector. Labour Day does yield a stronger empirical reference after
fine OD matching, but the fitted chain remains near chance and multistart
selection removes the small zero-init lift.
\item A fitting-free $2$-step backoff audit on the same Labour-Day split
also fails to lift the empirical reference: the best setting is the
degenerate $\lambda=0$ backoff, equivalent to the original $1$-step
chain. The SUMO route-memory result therefore motivates a future model
class, but it is not by itself an explanation of the Xuancheng failure.
\end{itemize}
Under controlled labelled simulation the method recovers a robust
fraction of the per-row chain contrast. On a real-world behavioural
proxy, the fitted $1$-step detector remains near chance once
population-mix is controlled.

\subsection{Limits of the chosen approach}
\label{sec:disc-limits}

Three limitations follow from the validation tiers.

\paragraph{Multi-seed Tier 2; limited real-world proxy pools on Tier 3.} The Tier~2
features result has been confirmed across five SUMO seeds
(\cref{sec:phase4-multiseed}), and that confirmation materially revised
the single-seed interpretation: the aggregate detection-AUC lift is
\emph{not} statistically robust ($+0.024 \pm 0.026$, reversing on one of
five seeds, $p \approx 0.11$), whereas the per-row chain-recovery lift
\emph{is} ($+0.050 \pm 0.023$, positive on all five, $p \approx 0.008$).
The multi-seed sweep also reproduced the bimodal basin structure,
confirming the cross-seed AUC variance is L-BFGS basin-selection rather
than measurement noise; a follow-up multistart check
(\cref{sec:phase4-multistart}) further established that this low basin is
\emph{not} escapable by random re-initialisation. Zero-init is the best
optimum found on both low-basin seeds, so the bimodality is a property of
the loss landscape, not of the starting point. Tier~3 (Xuancheng), by
contrast, now uses two behavioural proxy pools (rush-vs-off-peak weekdays
and Labour-Day-vs-normal days) but still lacks independent city/month
replication. A multi-pool or multi-city replication is the analogous
outstanding confirmation there, a compute-cost and data-availability
trade-off rather than a methodological barrier.

\paragraph{L-BFGS basin selection on \code{features=real}.} The
Xuancheng OD-matched \code{features=real} rush-chain fit
hit final loss $1397$, against the \code{features=none} fit's loss
$4.058$ on the same data, and produced the ``impossible''
$\AUC_{\mathrm{fit}} = 0.530 > \AUC_{\mathrm{emp}} = 0.518$. We ran the
multistart variant (\cref{sec:phase5-multistart}, $K=5$): it escaped the
high-loss basin (rush loss $1397 \rightarrow 9.186$, validation-selected),
and the correctly-fit chains then gave $\AUC_{\mathrm{fit}} = 0.483$ --
chance. The ceiling violation was therefore an optimiser artefact, and
removing it \emph{strengthens} the Tier-3 negative result: the within-OD
routing contrast is not detectable at the $1$-step Markov level under
this proxy and data split, rather than being masked by a bad optimum. This also makes the SUMO and Xuancheng basin
checks complementary. On SUMO zero-init was already the best optimum
(\cref{sec:phase4-multistart}); on Xuancheng a better optimum existed but
only confirmed the negative result, so neither tier's headline rests
on an optimiser accident.

\paragraph{The rush-vs-off-peak proxy confound, bounded in
simulation.} The Tier~3 proxy bundles two effects that real data
cannot separate: sat-navigation adoption (higher at rush hour) and
congestion-induced rerouting that affects all drivers when demand
rises. The Tier~2 $\rho \times d$ decomposition
(\cref{sec:phase4-rho-demand}) addresses this: holding $\rho=0$ across
the demand axis gives an empirical AUC of $0.493 \pm 0.018$ (within
noise of chance), while holding demand at peak across the
$\rho$ axis gives $0.780 \pm 0.013$. The full proxy diagonal
($0.793 \pm 0.023$) attributes $\sim\!96\%$ of its above-chance signal
to sat-navigation adoption, with the pure-congestion contribution
within noise and a small residual interaction. The decomposition holds
across all five SUMO seeds. The remaining caveat is that SUMO's
$\rho=0$ vehicles follow precomputed shortest-path routes and do not
react to congestion en route, so the SUMO pure-congestion AUC is a
\emph{conservative simulator estimate} that may understate the
experienced-human-driver confound; even tripling it would not change the
qualitative attribution. Real-world
verification of this decomposition would require an alternative
experienced-driver routing model in SUMO or, ideally, a real-world
dataset with sat-navigation-use labels.

\paragraph{The hitting-rate ($f+g$) extension at scale, refined
diagnosis.} The $f+g$ extension initially looked infeasible at SUMO
scale on the strength of a $9.5$-hour dense fit
that did not terminate. A robust diagnosis
(\cref{sec:phase4-fg-infeasible}) separates that single claim into
three: (i)~the engineering bottleneck is real but more subtle than
``replace dense with sparse''. A FD-verified sparse-LU
implementation now exists, but at SUMO scale ($d \approx 8{,}400$ wide
gradient RHS) dense LAPACK GETRS still wins by $\sim 80\times$ via
BLAS3 multi-core kernels; (ii)~the binding sensitivity is
$\gamma$, not the solver: at $\gamma = 0.5$ the floor-dominated $L_h$
overwhelms $L_f$ and $f+g$ hurts, while at $\gamma = 0.95$ on a
$200$-edge Xuancheng subgraph $f+g$ recovers the full-observation
empirical reference within sampling noise; (iii)~the data-density
issue ($\sim 85\%$ of $\gobs$ cells at floor) persists at every scale
we tested, so any future $f+g$ scale-up must operate at high $\gamma$.
Under the simpler $f$-only loss the framework remains tractable and
that is what the practical detector in this work uses. The natural
algorithmic next step is Sherman--Morrison amortisation of the
$(I - \beta P_T)$ factor across $X_o$ (one factor per L-BFGS iteration
instead of $|X_o|$) or the paper's natural-gradient descent
\cite{morimura2013}~$\S 4.1$; both are flagged as future work.

\subsection{Social, environmental, technological implications}
\label{sec:disc-implications}

\paragraph{Transport-policy modelling.} If externally-influenced routing
constitutes even a few percent of urban traffic flow, demand models
that assume revealed-preference inputs reflect intrinsic preferences
are systematically misspecified. A method that quantifies and filters
the externally-influenced fraction is a methodological prerequisite
for any policy intervention (congestion pricing, infrastructure
investment, route-recommendation regulation) whose evaluation rests on
preference-revealed data.

\paragraph{Privacy and data access.} The most-restrictive privacy
control among the three real-world AVI datasets we surveyed is also
the one that gives the strongest scientific guarantees: the Padua
deposit \cite{cappi2026} releases only aggregated flow statistics.
The least-restrictive is the Ma~\emph{et al.}\ Xuancheng release
\cite{ma2026xuancheng} which publishes joinable per-trip records with
anonymised IDs, enabling the per-trajectory detector this work
relies on, but at the cost of stronger reidentification surface area.
Methodologies that can validate per-trajectory detection from
aggregate-only data would shift this trade-off favourably.

\paragraph{Sat-navigation regulation.} The current sat-navigation
recommendation pipeline is unregulated in most jurisdictions; the
welfare effects of system-wide rerouting around congestion are
contested. A practical detector that can statistically attribute trip
fractions to sat-navigation guidance would underpin both academic
welfare analyses and possible regulatory disclosure requirements.

\subsection{Future work}
\label{sec:disc-future}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{OD-cell bootstrap.} A stratified vehicle-level bootstrap
confirms the fitted Tier-3 detector is statistically indistinguishable
from chance, and an OD-zone granularity sweep $K \in \{8, 16, 32, 64\}$
confirms this holds at every resolution (\cref{tab:phase5-ksweep}); the
sweep also showed the $K=8$ ``$\sim\!75\%$ OD-mix'' interpretation to be a
coarse-zoning artefact. The remaining outstanding extension is an
OD-\emph{cell} bootstrap that resamples which cells enter the match and
refits per replicate, quantifying cell-selection variance on top of
vehicle-sampling variance, a compute-time rather than methodological
gap.
\item \textbf{Observation-station sensitivity.} All headline Tier~2 and
Tier~3 fitted-chain results condition on one random observation-station
set $\Xo$ at the stated observation fraction. This matches the main
validation protocol but understates uncertainty for real deployments,
where detector performance may depend on which camera or loop-detector
locations are available. A full sensitivity analysis would repeat the
inverse-MC fits over multiple $X_o$ seeds, report the induced spread in
AUC and per-row Pearson, and combine this with the vehicle-level
bootstrap above. This is a refit-level experiment, not something the
existing bootstrap scores can recover after the fact.
\item \textbf{Trajectory-level feature augmentation.} The strict
$1$-step Markov LR detector ignores trip-level features (length, mean
speed, edge-class composition, dwell time at congested edges). A
logistic-regression layer on top of $\Lam(\tau)$ that adds those
features would lift detection AUC further at the cost of a slightly
weaker chain-recovery claim. Worth doing as a clearly-labelled
``deployment-oriented'' extension.
\item \textbf{Sherman--Morrison factor sharing in $\bm{f+g}$.} The
sparse-LU implementation (\cref{sec:phase4-fg-infeasible}) is
FD-verified but slower than dense at SUMO scale because the wide
gradient RHS $V \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$ benefits from BLAS3
multi-core kernels. The natural amortisation is to factor
$(I - \beta P_T)$ \emph{once} per L-BFGS iteration and derive each
per-state $h_j$ and $\nabla h_j$ via a Sherman--Morrison update
(rank-1 in the row of $P_T$ being zeroed out), which converts
$|X_o| = 601$ factor + wide-solve pairs into a single factor and
a single wide-solve plus $|X_o|$ rank-1 corrections. This is the
algorithmic step that bridges the engineering gap exposed in the
subgraph experiment.

\item \textbf{Natural-gradient or LM optimisation.} Per
\cref{sec:phase4-fg-infeasible}, even with the engineering fix above,
the $f+g$ loss surface is under-constrained under floor-dominated
$\gobs$ and benefits from manifold-aware steps. The paper's
$\S 4.1$ natural-gradient descent or a Levenberg--Marquardt
formulation against the residual Jacobian (a preliminary implementation
was abandoned at $d = 8{,}392$ for memory cost) would
combine well with Sherman--Morrison and enable an
$f+g$-with-features re-validation at city scale.
\item \textbf{OD-conditional fitting on Xuancheng.} The
OD-rebalancing extension matches the marginal $(\text{zone}_o,
\text{zone}_d)$ distribution. A stronger control fits per-cell chains
and aggregates the per-cell contrasts; smaller sample sizes per cell
but cleaner methodologically. The loader infrastructure required
already exists.
\item \textbf{Parametric 2-step Morimura extension.} The regularised
2-step empirical of \cref{sec:phase4-2step} lifts AUC by about
$+0.09$--$+0.10$ across five SUMO seeds; the selected
count-conditional setting gives $+0.100 \pm 0.009$ but is mildly
test-tuned. A matched Xuancheng Labour-Day audit does \emph{not} show
the same lift: non-zero $2$-step interpolation lowers AUC relative to the
$1$-step empirical reference. The natural inverter extension that the
SUMO bound motivates is still a parametric softmax on $(x_p, x)$
contexts, but it should be treated as a simulation-motivated model-class
extension rather than the confirmed remedy for Xuancheng. The
state-space expansion is significant
($|X|^2 \approx 5.8\text{M}$ pairs) but only $\sim\!3{,}500$ are
observed in training, so the parameter count and gradient cost grow as
the observed-pair count, not as $n^2$. Per-pair softmax parameters
with feature-sharing across topologically similar pairs would be the
first design to try.

\item \textbf{Experienced-driver routing model in SUMO.} The Tier~2
$\rho \times d$ decomposition (\cref{sec:phase4-rho-demand}) bounds the
congestion-routing confound from below, but its $\rho=0$ vehicles
follow precomputed shortest-path routes and do not react to congestion
en route. A SUMO scenario in which unaided drivers carry coarse
local congestion knowledge (e.g.\ time-of-day-conditioned edge
attractiveness) would tighten the bound to a quantity that more
closely tracks human experienced-driver behaviour. SUMO's routing
infrastructure supports this; an off-the-shelf alternative is
\code{rerouting-with-static-traffic-class} priors keyed to rush-hour
edge usage.
\end{enumerate}
