\section{Tier 1: Synthetic Reproduction of the Morimura Benchmark}
\label{sec:phase1-synthetic}

This tier establishes that our implementation of the inverter is a
faithful reference. We replicate the $\S 6.1$ synthetic experiment of
\citet{morimura2013} end-to-end and verify that we reach the published
RMAE curve to within $\sim 0.05$ across observation fractions in
$[0.10, 0.90]$.

\subsection{Experimental setup}

A synthetic chain is constructed on $n=100$ states with a random
strongly-connected adjacency, mean out-degree $\sim 3$. The true
transition kernel is a softmax of \cref{eq:softmax} with all parameters
($\nu$, $\omega^{\mathrm{loc}}$, $\omega^{\mathrm{glo}_1}$,
$\omega^{\mathrm{glo}_2}$) drawn iid $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$, with $d_T = d_\psi = 5$
random global features per the paper. The truth is then mixed
$70/30$ with a Dirichlet$(0.3)$ noise row-distribution to push it
slightly outside the parametric family ($\sim 4\%$ TV distance) and so
present the inverter with a model-class gap analogous to a real-world
scenario.

For each observation fraction $|\Xo|/n \in \{0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.35,
0.50, 0.70, 0.90\}$, $X_o$ is randomly sampled, and the inverter is
fitted on the analytic $\fobs$ and $\gobs$ derived from the true chain
at $X_o$, with Tikhonov regularisation $\lambda$ cross-validated over
$\{10^{-4}, 10^{-3}, 10^{-2}, 10^{-1}\}$ on a $75/25$ split of $X_o$.
The reported metric is the relative mean absolute error
\begin{equation}
\RMAE(\hat{\pi}, \pi) \;=\; \frac{\frac{1}{|\mathcal{X} \setminus X_o|}\sum_{x \notin X_o}|\hat{\pi}(x) - \pi(x)|}{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}}\pi(x)},
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the mean absolute error on unobserved states normalised by the
average true stationary mass. The recovered $\hat{\pi}$ is rescaled by a
scale-free convention discussed in \cref{sec:phase1-scaling-gotcha}.
Each $|X_o|$ value is repeated for $10$ trials with fresh truth +
$X_o$ draws, matching the visible error-bar count in the original
Fig.~2A.

tracks the paper's Fig.~2A within $\sim 0.05$ RMAE across $|X_o| \in
[10, 90]$. \cref{tab:phase1-rmae} summarises.

The single value where our number is materially off the paper's is
$|X_o|=5$, where our $\RMAE \approx 0.97$ against the paper's
$\sim 0.7$. Investigation revealed this is a $\lambda$-CV degeneracy:
with $|X_o| = 5$ states, the $75/25$ split puts $\approx 1$ state in the
validation set, which is not enough to discriminate $\lambda$ values
robustly, and the cross-validated $\lambda$ over-regularises. The
Nadaraya--Watson kernel-regression baseline (``NWKR'') does not drop
as steeply as the paper's, which we attribute to a comparator-
implementation gap rather than a method error.

\subsection{Implementation gotcha: prediction rescaling}
\label{sec:phase1-scaling-gotcha}

The paper writes the recovered prediction as $\hat{f}(x) = \hat{c}\,\hat{\pi}(x)$
with $\hat{c} = \overline{f}_{X_o}$ (their $\S 6.1$). Taken literally
this collapses every RMAE to $\approx 1$ because \code{stationary()}
returns a probability summing to $1$ over $n$ states (so
$\hat{\pi}(x) \sim 1/n$ while $f(x) \sim K/n$), under-scaling by a
factor of $n$. The operationally correct convention --- and the one
that yields the paper's reported numbers --- is the scale-free form
$\hat{f}(x) = (\sum_{X_o}\!\fobs)\,\hat{\pi}(x) / (\sum_{X_o}\!\hat{\pi})$,
which reduces to the literal formula when $\sum_{X_o}\!\hat{\pi} = 1$.
We verified this empirically: applying the literal formula collapses
all RMAE values to $\approx 1.0$. The scale-free form is preserved
throughout the rest of this work.

\subsection{Gradient verification}

Across three random parameter draws including local-only,
$\phi_T$-only, and $\psi$-only edge-case configurations, the centred
finite-difference gradient check returns maximum relative error
$\le 5 \times 10^{-9}$ for every block, confirming the
analytic derivation. Details of the FD check infrastructure are in
\cref{sec:methodology} and \code{morimura.py}.

\subsection{Headline outcome}

The synthetic reproduction is locked in as a faithful reference. The
curve shape and method ordering both match the paper. This implementation
serves as the upstream operational reference for all subsequent
validation tiers; downstream limitations in Tier~2 and Tier~3 are
attributable to the data and the model class at scale, not to the
upstream framework as implemented here.
