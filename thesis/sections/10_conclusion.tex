\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This dissertation designed, validated, and stress-tested a
\emph{partial-observation routing-influence filter}: a pipeline that
ingests a road graph, regime-labelled partial observations at a sparse
subset of stations $\Xo$, and trajectories, and emits a per-trip
log-likelihood-ratio score, a thresholded filter decision, and a set of
diagnostics. The inverse-Markov-chain method of Morimura et
al.~\cite{morimura2013}, originally a single-chain recovery procedure,
sits inside the filter as the estimator; the three experimental
tiers exist to justify, parameterise, and audit specific filter design
choices using a single shared scoring core.

\paragraph{What was designed.} A filter with (i) an FD-verified
inverse-Markov-chain estimator, including the omega-global gradients
that the paper states but does not derive; (ii) a feature-regularised
filter variant that wires SUMO-derived edge attributes (road class,
speed, lane count, turn geometry) into the estimator as $10$ shared
global parameters; (iii) a per-trajectory log-likelihood-ratio scoring
rule with a deployment-tunable decision threshold $\eta$; (iv) a
diagnostic suite comprising a per-row Pearson/cosine contrast against a
full-observation empirical reference, an OD-rebalancing pre-step for
real-world population-mix control, multistart basin checks, and a
fitting-free empirical $2$-step memory audit.

\paragraph{How it works.} The estimator fits one regime kernel per
regime by minimising the regularised log-ratio loss of
\cite{morimura2013}; the scorer accumulates $\log
\hat{p}_B(x_{t+1}\!\mid\!x_t) - \log \hat{p}_A(x_{t+1}\!\mid\!x_t)$
along each trajectory and thresholds the result; the diagnostic suite
flags, before any downstream claim is made, whether the AUC
reflects per-row chain recovery or aggregate bias, whether OD-mix has
been controlled at the chosen granularity, and whether the chosen
optimum survives a multistart audit.

\paragraph{What worked.} The synthetic tier (\cref{sec:phase1-synthetic})
validates the estimator inside the filter: the reproduction matches the
published proposed-method curve closely for $|\Xo| \ge 10$, with
gradients FD-verified at machine precision on non-zero components.
Under controlled SUMO simulation, the full filter reaches $\AUC = 0.708$
at $T = 40$ against a full-observation empirical $1$-step reference of
$0.801$, and the feature-regularised filter variant robustly improves
per-row chain recovery across a five-seed sweep (stacked Pearson
$+0.050 \pm 0.023$, $p \approx 0.008$, positive on all five seeds). The
pipeline ports unchanged at its core to a $1{,}733$-edge real-world
network.

\paragraph{What failed or was limited.} The aggregate detection-AUC
lift from features is seed-dependent, reversing on one of five seeds,
so the per-row diagnostic re-classifies features as a chain-recovery
lever rather than a robust detection lever. This is exactly the kind
of design-relevant verdict the audit suite exists to enable. Conversely, a properly regularised
empirical $2$-step audit (interpolation backoff with count-conditional
trust) lifts the AUC by about $+0.09$--$+0.10$ across five SUMO seeds,
with every seed winning; the selected count-conditional setting gives
$+0.100 \pm 0.009$ but is mildly test-tuned. This identifies
higher-order routing memory as a real signal the $1$-step softmax family
cannot capture, and promotes a parametric $2$-step Morimura extension to
a justified future direction. The hitting-rate ($f+g$) route does not naively transfer to full-SUMO
scale: a sparse-LU rewrite is FD-verified and works, but at $|X_o|
\approx 600$ the wide gradient solve still favours dense BLAS3 over
sparse per-column solves by $\sim 80\times$, and a Sherman--Morrison
amortisation would be the right algorithmic next step. At a sub-network
scale where the framework is operationally fine, $f+g$ at the
correctly-tuned loss weight ($\gamma \approx 0.95$) lifts AUC by
$+0.055$ and matches the full-observation reference on the test
subgraph within sampling noise; the $\sim 85\%$ floor-dominated
empirical $g$ persists at every scale we tested and is the
$\gamma$-tuning constraint. On the real AVI data the
fitted $1$-step filter does not recover the rush-vs-off-peak contrast
at any OD-zone granularity tested ($K \in \{8, 16, 32, 64\}$; AUC
$0.476$--$0.513$, every bootstrap CI straddling chance), even though
the full-observation reference weakly carries it
($\sim\!0.55$--$0.58$ for $K \ge 16$); the multistart audit confirms
this is a genuine near-chance outcome rather than an optimiser
artefact. An OD-mix attribution we initially inferred from the coarse $K=8$
match ($\sim\!75\%$) did not survive the granularity sweep and is not
claimed.

\paragraph{Where the filter is reliable.} Under controlled, labelled
simulation with shared OD pairs across regimes, the filter recovers a
robust fraction of the per-row routing contrast and supplies a
calibrated AUC together with audit signals that correctly distinguish
chain recovery from aggregate bias. The Tier-2 SUMO result is the
strongest defensible claim that this dissertation makes about the
filter as a positive detector.

\paragraph{Where the filter is not reliable.} On a real-world
behavioural proxy without ground-truth sat-navigation labels, the
within-OD routing signal, once OD-mix is controlled, is not
detectable at the $1$-step Markov level under the tested proxy, model
class, and data split. The filter therefore does \emph{not} support
real-world sat-navigation detection on the Xuancheng dataset; Tier~3 is
reported as a confounding audit, not a positive detection result.
The filter's optional $\gobs$ input is fully tractable at the
sub-network scale where its empirical estimate is informative:
there it lifts AUC and recovers the empirical reference within
sampling noise. It does not currently transfer to a full city
network under naive sparse-LU; a Sherman--Morrison factor-sharing
update (or the paper's natural-gradient descent) is the algorithmic
prerequisite for re-enabling the $f+g$ mode at city scale.

\paragraph{Contribution.} The contribution is a designed and audited
filter pipeline, not a fresh estimator. Academically, the work extends
the Morimura framework into a trajectory-level regime detector and
supplies diagnostics that distinguish chain recovery from aggregate
bias, along with a controlled-simulator decomposition
(\cref{sec:phase4-rho-demand}) that bounds the sat-navigation-vs.-congestion
confound in the rush-vs-off-peak behavioural proxy used on real-world
data, documenting where the approach succeeds (controlled simulation
with labels) and where it does not (real-world proxy without labels,
OD-mix confounding, $1$-step model class, $f+g$ engineering gap at
city scale). Practically, it shows that quantifying externally-influenced
routing on real urban data is gated less by the inverse-MC machinery
than by the availability of ground-truth adoption labels and by control
for OD-mix confounding, a result that directly informs how any
future real-world sat-navigation detector should be scoped, evaluated,
and audited.
