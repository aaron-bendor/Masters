\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This work adapts the Morimura, Osogami and Id\'e inverse-Markov-chain
framework \cite{morimura2013} --- originally a single-chain recovery method
--- into a two-regime detection pipeline, and validates it across a
synthetic reproduction, a controlled SUMO simulation, and a real-world
Xuancheng AVI dataset using a single shared scoring core.

\paragraph{What was built.} A faithful, FD-verified re-implementation of the
inverter (including the omega-global gradients the paper states but does not
derive), a per-trajectory log-likelihood-ratio detector, a contrast-correlation
diagnostic that separates per-row chain recovery from aggregate-bias detection,
an empirical $2$-step diagnostic, an OD-rebalancing loader for real-world
population-mix control, and multistart basin checks for optimiser robustness.

\paragraph{What worked.} The synthetic tier reproduces the proposed-method
curve closely for $|\Xo| \ge 10$. On controlled SUMO data, real edge features
robustly improve per-row chain recovery (stacked Pearson $+0.050 \pm 0.023$,
$p \approx 0.008$, positive on all five seeds), and the detector reaches
$\AUC = 0.708$ at $T = 40$ against a full-observation empirical $1$-step
reference of $0.801$. The pipeline ports unchanged at its core to a
$1{,}733$-edge real network.

\paragraph{What failed or was limited.} The aggregate detection-AUC lift from
features is seed-dependent, reversing on one of five seeds, so features are a
chain-recovery lever rather than a robust detection lever. The empirical
$2$-step detector does not improve over the $1$-step reference, weakening the
case for a parametric $2$-step extension as the immediate next step. The $f+g$
hitting-rate route is computationally infeasible at SUMO scale because the
empirical $g$ is floor-dominated. On the real AVI data, OD-mix explains roughly
three-quarters of the apparent rush-versus-off-peak chain difference; after
OD-matching, the remaining within-OD $1$-step Markov signal is close to chance,
and a multistart check confirms this is a genuine near-chance result rather
than an optimiser artefact.

\paragraph{Strongest defensible claim.} Under controlled, labelled simulation,
an inverse-MC regime detector with real network features recovers a robust
fraction of the per-row routing contrast; on a real-world behavioural proxy
without ground-truth labels, the within-OD routing signal is not detectable
under the tested proxy, model class, and data split once population-mix is
controlled. The work does \emph{not} claim to detect sat-navigation use in the
real world --- it lacks the labels to do so --- and Tier~3 is reported as a
confounding audit, not a positive detection result.

\paragraph{Contribution.} Academically, the work establishes both the
potential and the limits of $1$-step inverse-Markov-chain methods under partial
observation: it extends the Morimura framework to regime detection, supplies
diagnostics that distinguish chain recovery from aggregate bias, and documents
where the approach breaks (model class, optimiser, labels, confounding).
Practically, it shows that quantifying externally-influenced routing on
real urban data is gated less by the inverse-MC machinery than by the
availability of ground-truth adoption labels and by control for OD-mix
confounding --- a result that directly informs how any future real-world
sat-navigation detector should be scoped and evaluated.
