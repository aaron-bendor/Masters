\section{Tier 3: Real-world stress test of the filter under OD confounding}
\label{sec:phase5-xuancheng}

This tier stress-tests the partial-observation routing-influence filter
(defined in \cref{sec:intro-filter}) against a real-world urban
automatic vehicle identification (AVI) dataset from Xuancheng, China
\cite{ma2026xuancheng}, with the OD-rebalancing audit
(\cref{sec:od-rebal}) activated. The estimator, the scoring rule, and
the per-row diagnostic are reused unchanged from Tier~2; what changes are
the loader, the regime definition, and the OD-control logic. Because no
real dataset carries ground-truth sat-navigation adoption labels, this
tier cannot directly validate sat-navigation detection. It is a
real-world \emph{confounding audit}
that asks whether a plausible behavioural proxy (rush-hour vs.\
off-peak travel) contains a within-OD route-choice signal once the
population-mix confound is controlled, and whether the filter survives
that confounding. As the results below show, under the tested proxy the
within-OD contrast is not recovered. The absence of a ground-truth
label is the defining methodological constraint of this tier, and the
filter's OD-rebalancing and multistart audits exist precisely so that
this constraint can be reported directly rather than masked by a
spurious AUC.

\subsection{Dataset choice}\label{sec:phase5-dataset}

Three candidate datasets were evaluated. The Padua AVI deposit
\cite{cappi2026} releases only aggregated $10$-minute flow statistics
and pairwise travel times over $40$ junctions; per-vehicle trajectories
are stripped at the source pipeline for privacy and cannot be
reconstructed downstream. The Wang \emph{et al.}\ Xuancheng deposit
\cite{wang2023xuancheng} explicitly restricts raw trajectory access
behind a supervised-interactive interface. The
Ma et al.~\cite{ma2026xuancheng} Xuancheng release, by contrast, publishes
joinable entry/exit tables with anonymised vehicle identifiers from
which complete per-vehicle trajectories can be reconstructed downstream;
this is the dataset we use. \cref{tab:phase5-dataset-scale} summarises
its scale.

\subsection{Verification: route legality and time-of-day distribution}
\label{sec:phase5-verification}

Two non-obvious data-quality issues affect how the loader processes
trips, each of which was identified before any fitting was attempted:

\paragraph{Route legality.} Of all consecutive edge pairs $(x_t,
x_{t+1})$ across the trip routes, only $78.4\%$ satisfy the SUMO net's
lane-connection definition $x_{t+1} \in \mathrm{adj}(x_t)$. The
remaining $21.6\%$ split as $\sim 1.9\%$ junction-adjacent-but-disconnected
(a definitional gap in the converted SUMO net) and $\sim 19.7\%$
inter-junction ``teleports'' attributable to the source pipeline's
Dijkstra-repair step that fills in missing AVI passages on
CityFlow's connectivity graph rather than the SUMO net's. The loader
handles this by \emph{splitting trips at illegal boundaries} into
legal-only sub-trajectories rather than dropping the trip wholesale:
a $15$-edge raw trip with one illegal jump becomes two sub-trajectories.
This is the conservative choice because the inverse-MC scorer assigns
likelihood only to legal successors in $\mathrm{adj}(x)$; treating
source-pipeline repair jumps as real road transitions would contaminate
the fitted chain with edges that do not exist in the analysed network.
The cost is that Tier~3 is a test on legalised sub-trajectories rather
than raw AVI trips. Mean sub-trajectory length is $8.2$ edges, and the
$T=10$ experiments therefore draw their test set from the subset of
sub-trajectories with at least $11$ edges, selecting the longer and
cleaner fragments of the reconstructed data.

\paragraph{Time-of-day distribution.} Across all $30$ daily files, the
trip \code{startTime} field is seconds-since-midnight in $[0, 86400]$
with a clear AM-rush + PM-rush + midday-off-peak structure. We use this
to construct a rush-vs-off-peak regime split with windows
$\text{rush} = [25{,}200, 32{,}400] \cup [61{,}200, 68{,}400]$
(7--9~AM and 5--7~PM) and
$\text{offpeak} = [36{,}000, 57{,}600]$ (10~AM--4~PM); trips outside
either window are discarded. Three anomalous days are flagged for
exclusion or special handling: Apr~5 (Tomb-Sweeping holiday, $264$k
trips, demand outlier), Apr~28--29 (Labour Day eve, $\sim 425$k trips),
Apr~10 (\code{startTime} max~$\approx 165$k, suggesting a multi-day
concatenation), and Apr~8/9 (identical trip counts of $324{,}441$ in both
files, almost certainly a duplicate).

\subsection{The fundamental real-world gap: no ground-truth sat-nav label}
\label{sec:phase5-proxy}

Tier~2 used \code{--device.rerouting.probability} as ground truth: every
vehicle either does or does not use sat-navigation, and the regime split
is exact by construction. No real-world AVI dataset publishes this
label. The closest behavioural proxy available in Xuancheng is the
rush-vs-off-peak time-of-day contrast: if external rerouting influence
is triggered by congestion, then rush-hour trips should differ from
off-peak trips in routing choices, and the inverse-MC detector should
detect that difference.

This proxy is weaker than the SUMO contrast in two ways. First, rush
and off-peak trips have \emph{different populations} (commuters vs.\
discretionary trips), so any chain difference reflects both the
routing-choice effect (which we want) and the OD-mix effect (which
we do not). Second, the actual sat-navigation adoption rate in
Xuancheng is presumably much lower than the $100\%$ used in SUMO, so
even the routing-choice signal is diluted by the unguided fraction.

We address the OD-mix confound directly via the OD-rebalancing
extension of \cref{sec:od-rebal}. We cannot address the lower-adoption
dilution from observation alone; what we can do is report the resulting
real-world AUC as a lower bound on the method's sensitivity
in deployment.

A further concern is that the rush-vs-off-peak contrast bundles
\emph{two} potentially confounding effects: sat-navigation adoption
(higher at rush hour) \emph{and} congestion-induced rerouting that
affects all drivers (sat-nav users and unaided drivers alike) when
demand rises. Real data cannot separate these without
sat-navigation-use labels. The Tier~2 $\rho \times d$ decomposition
(\cref{sec:phase4-rho-demand}) provides a partial answer: in the
SUMO model, varying congestion at $\rho=0$ produces an empirical AUC
of $0.493 \pm 0.018$ (statistically indistinguishable from chance),
whereas varying $\rho$ at fixed peak congestion produces
$0.780 \pm 0.013$, and the full rush-vs-off-peak proxy diagonal
($0.793 \pm 0.023$) attributes $\sim\!96\%$ of its above-chance signal
to sat-navigation adoption, with the rest as within-noise residual
across five SUMO seeds. This validates the proxy as a sat-navigation
proxy in the controlled tier and bounds the congestion-routing
confound, though with the residual caveat that SUMO's $\rho=0$ drivers
do not react to congestion en route, so the lower-bound character of
the bound does not transfer fully to experienced human drivers.

Two conclusions follow. First, the ``$\sim\!75\%$ is OD-mix'' decomposition
that the $K=8$ row alone would support is \emph{not robust}: the implied
OD-mix fraction ranges from $\sim\!75\%$ at $K=8$ to $\approx 0\%$ at
$K=64$, where matching leaves the reference statistically unchanged from
the un-matched pool. The coarse $K=8$ matching appears to
\emph{over-correct} by aggregating heterogeneous sub-flows into $8$
macro-zones and equalising them washes out genuine within-OD routing
contrast along with OD-mix, whereas at finer resolutions a small but
significant within-OD rush-vs-off-peak contrast survives
($\AUC_{\mathrm{emp}} \approx 0.55$--$0.58$, CIs excluding chance at
$K \ge 16$). We therefore do not retain the strong ``three-quarters is
OD-mix'' interpretation; it is an artefact of a single coarse zone choice.
Second, and this
is the result that \emph{is} robust, the fitted $1$-step inverse-MC
detector remains indistinguishable from chance at every $K$
($0.476$--$0.513$, every CI straddling $0.50$): the method does not
recover the within-OD contrast at any tested OD-zone granularity, even
where full observation weakly does.

A secondary observation: in the OD-matched row, $\AUC_{\mathrm{fit}}$
under \code{features=real} ($0.530$) numerically exceeds
$\AUC_{\mathrm{emp}}$ ($0.518$). Under correct chain recovery this is
impossible, since the full-observation empirical chains should upper-bound
any partial-observation fit. The suspected cause was the
L-BFGS basin-selection pathology also seen on the un-matched pool
(rush-fit loss $1397$ at \code{features=real} vs.\ $4.058$ at
\code{features=none}, a $\sim 340\times$ ratio). A multistart
$K=5$ run (\cref{sec:phase5-multistart}) confirms it: random
re-initialisation \emph{escapes} the bad basin: the rush-chain loss
drops from $1397$ to $9.186$ at a validation-selected restart, and the
properly-fit chains then give $\AUC_{\mathrm{fit}} = 0.483$, i.e.\ they
fall \emph{back to chance} and no longer violate the empirical ceiling.
The $0.530$ was therefore an optimiser artefact, not a real signal; the
genuine within-OD routing contrast under correct chain recovery is
indistinguishable from chance. This strengthens rather than weakens the
Tier-3 negative result: the apparent ``features help on real data''
interpretation was a basin coincidence, and the per-row Pearson on
\texttt{[all branching]} of $+0.077$ (real) vs $+0.104$ (none) already
pointed the same way: features do not improve chain recovery on this
real-world loss landscape.

\subsection{Interpretation}\label{sec:phase5-interpretation}

Three findings are worth foregrounding from \cref{tab:phase5-headline}.

\paragraph{The OD-mix attribution is not robust to zone granularity.}
At $K=8$ the full-observation reference falls from $0.573$ to $0.518$,
which taken alone would attribute $\sim\!75\%$ of the above-chance
contrast to OD-mix. The granularity sweep (\cref{tab:phase5-ksweep})
shows this figure is an artefact of coarse zoning: at $K \ge 16$ the
OD-matched reference returns to $0.55$--$0.58$, statistically above
chance and close to the un-matched $0.573$, so fine-grained OD control
removes almost none of the contrast. We therefore do \emph{not} claim a
stable OD-mix fraction. The defensible statements are narrower: coarse
macro-zone matching can over-correct (washing out within-OD signal along
with OD-mix), and once zones are fine enough the rush-vs-off-peak
contrast is largely \emph{not} explained by OD-mix, consistent with
rush and off-peak trips sharing very similar fine-grained
origin-destination patterns (the matched and un-matched pools differ by
$<25{,}000$ of $\sim\!520{,}000$ trips per regime at $K=64$).

\paragraph{The fitted $1$-step detector does not recover the contrast at
any granularity.} This is the robust Tier-3 finding. Across
$K \in \{8, 16, 32, 64\}$ the fitted partial-observation detector stays
at chance ($\AUC_{\mathrm{fit}} \in [0.476, 0.513]$, every bootstrap
$95\%$ CI straddling $0.50$; \cref{tab:phase5-ksweep}), even though the
full-observation reference shows a small but significant within-OD
contrast at $K \ge 16$. Per-row Pearson on \texttt{[all branching]} is
$\le 0.104$ in every Xuancheng configuration, against $+0.215$ on SUMO
under labelled comparison. So whatever within-OD rush-vs-off-peak
structure the full-observation chains carry, the $1$-step inverse-MC
method under partial observation ($|X_o| = 433$ of $1{,}733$) does not
recover it at any tested OD-zone resolution. The stratified
vehicle-level bootstrap ($B = 2{,}000$, drawn with replacement within
each regime) supports the chance-level interpretation: at $K=8$ the empirical CI
is $[0.471, 0.567]$ ($P(\AUC>0.5)=0.78$) and the fitted CI $[0.429,
0.524]$, and the fitted detector's CI straddles chance at every finer
$K$ as well. These intervals are conditional on the fitted chains, the
fixed observation-station set, the fixed OD match, and the fixed
train/test split; they do not include station-selection or refit
variance. The outstanding statistical extensions are an
OD-\emph{cell} bootstrap (resampling which cells enter the match and
refitting per replicate) and an $X_o$-selection sensitivity sweep, both
left to future work because they require full refits rather than cheap
resampling of already-computed trajectory scores.

\paragraph{The features=real basin failure repeats post-matching, and
multistart resolves it.} Both the un-matched pool
(\cref{sec:phase5-results}) and the OD-matched pool show the same pattern:
the rush-fit \code{features=real} loss is $\sim 350\times$ the
\code{features=none} loss ($1397$ vs.\ $4.058$ here). The
$\AUC_{\mathrm{fit}} > \AUC_{\mathrm{emp}}$ behaviour in the matched row
suggested a basin-selection artefact rather than a chain-recovery
improvement. The multistart check of \cref{sec:phase5-multistart}
confirms exactly this: random restarts escape the high-loss basin (rush
loss $1397 \rightarrow 9.186$), and the resulting well-fit chains give
$\AUC_{\mathrm{fit}} = 0.483$, chance-level, resolving the apparent
ceiling violation.

\subsection{Multistart on \code{features=real}: the ceiling violation is
an optimiser artefact}\label{sec:phase5-multistart}

The one anomaly left open above is the OD-matched
$\AUC_{\mathrm{fit}} = 0.530 > \AUC_{\mathrm{emp}} = 0.518$ under
\code{features=real}, with its tell-tale $1397$ rush-chain loss. We ran
the multistart variant (\code{score\_seed\_big\_multistart.py},
$K=5$ random restarts $\theta_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)$ per chain,
validation-selected by held-out log-likelihood) on the exact OD-matched
\code{features=real} configuration ($d=6{,}136$, $T=10$,
$|X_o| = 433$, $517{,}177$ train transitions per regime).

The result is decisive and \emph{opposite} to the SUMO basin check of
\cref{sec:phase4-multistart}. On Xuancheng, multistart \emph{does} escape
the high-loss basin: the rush (intrinsic) chain loss drops from the
zero-init baseline of $1397$ to $9.186$ at the validation-selected
restart (restart losses $\{1413, 1844, 273.4, 9.186, 1792\}$, so one
restart of five found the good basin). But the well-fit chains then give
$\AUC_{\mathrm{fit}} = 0.483$, below chance, against the
baseline's spurious $0.530$. The per-init paired AUCs
($\{0.504, 0.492, 0.497, 0.494, 0.491\}$, mean $0.496$) cluster tightly
at chance regardless of which basin each restart lands in.

The interpretation closes two threads. First, the
$\AUC_{\mathrm{fit}} > \AUC_{\mathrm{emp}}$ ``impossible'' result was
indeed a basin artefact: a high-loss chain that drifted into a direction
producing a marginally favourable aggregate-bias signal. Once the
optimiser reaches the genuine optimum, that spurious signal disappears
and the detector returns to chance. Second, and this is the
load-bearing scientific point, the correctly-fit \code{features=real}
chains \emph{confirm} the central Tier-3 finding rather than overturning
it: the within-OD rush-vs-off-peak routing contrast is not detectable
at the $1$-step Markov level under this proxy and data split, not merely
hidden by a bad optimum. The
SUMO and Xuancheng basin checks are thus complementary: on SUMO,
zero-init was already the best optimum and the low basin was not an
artefact; on Xuancheng, multistart found a better optimum but it only
confirmed the negative result. Neither dataset's headline depends on an
optimiser accident.

\subsection{Bottom line for Tier 3}\label{sec:phase5-bottom}

The pipeline runs cleanly on real-world data. The framework's
gradient implementation, FD-check, regime-split machinery, and contrast
diagnostics all port to the Xuancheng dataset without modification. The
headline finding is robust to OD-zone granularity: \textbf{across
$K \in \{8, 16, 32, 64\}$ the fitted $1$-step inverse-MC detector does
not recover the rush-vs-off-peak contrast (AUC $0.476$--$0.513$, every
bootstrap CI straddling chance), even though the full-observation
reference weakly carries it ($\sim\!0.55$--$0.58$ for $K \ge 16$)}. A
secondary, \emph{granularity-sensitive} observation is that aggressive
coarse-zone matching ($K=8$) can over-correct the reference down to
chance ($0.518$); we report this but do not rest any claim on the
``$\sim\!75\%$ OD-mix'' decomposition it would imply, since that figure
collapses to $\approx 0\%$ at $K=64$. This is a methodologically strong
negative result for behavioural-proxy validation of sat-navigation
detection on real-world AVI data, and it characterises the gap between
the controlled-simulation regime (Tier~2, $0.708$ AUC on labelled
SUMO contrast) and the real-world regime under realistic
sat-navigation adoption rates.

The result also identifies the methodological lever any future
real-world validation of this framework needs: \emph{ground-truth
sat-navigation adoption labels}, even partial, on the underlying trip
records. With them, the proxy-weakness problem disappears and the
controlled-comparison machinery of Tier~2 transfers directly. Without
them, the fitted $1$-step detector cannot separate genuine within-OD
routing differences from chance on this behavioural proxy, at any OD-zone
granularity tested.
