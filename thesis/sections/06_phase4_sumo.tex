\section{Tier 2: Simulated Validation on SUMO}
\label{sec:phase4-sumo}

This tier is the controlled validation: an identical population of
vehicles is simulated once with sat-navigation rerouting turned off and
once with it turned on, producing a labelled comparison
against which the inverse-MC detector can be benchmarked. We construct
the scenario at city-block scale ($n = 2{,}405$ edges), apply real edge
features as global priors, characterise the model-class ceiling with the
$2$-step empirical diagnostic, and quantify the lift due to trajectory
length via a $T$-sweep. We close with a documented infeasibility of
the hitting-rate ($f+g$) extension at this scale.

\subsection{Scenario construction}\label{sec:phase4-scenario}

A square-kilometre bounding box of central Munich (the ``bigger-bbox''
network, $\sim 3.7\,\text{km} \times 3.3\,\text{km}$, derived from OSM via
\code{netconvert}) yields $n = 2{,}405$ directed edges after filtering
to the largest strongly-connected passenger-allowed component, with
$E = 5{,}987$ directed transitions and mean out-degree $\sim 2.5$. A
demand of $\sim 28{,}800$ vehicles is generated by
\code{randomTrips.py} over a four-hour horizon at $-p\,0.5$. Each vehicle
is replayed twice under the same insertion times and origin/destination
pairs:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Intrinsic regime:} \code{--device.rerouting.probability 0}.
Vehicles route via static shortest-path Dijkstra and never react to
congestion.
\item \textbf{Sat-navigation regime:} \code{--device.rerouting.probability 1}.
Every vehicle re-evaluates its route every $30\,\mathrm{s}$ on the basis
of observed live edge travel times.
\end{itemize}
Operating at $-p\,0.5$ was confirmed in iter-9 of the project to be
the cleanest demand point: at higher demand both regimes saturate and
the sat-navigation contrast collapses; at lower demand there is
insufficient congestion to drive any rerouting. At $-p\,0.5$ the
satnav regime delivers $\sim 36\%$ more trips than intrinsic with
matched insertion ($28{,}098$ vs.\ $20{,}700$ finishing).

For each regime we expose vehicle counts \code{edgedata.*.xml} (the
empirical $\fobs$) and complete per-vehicle routes \code{vehroutes.*.xml}
(used as the test set and to construct the empirical chain for the
contrast diagnostic). The inverter sees only $|X_o| = 601$ randomly
sampled edges --- $25\%$ of the network --- consistent with the
operating point of the Morimura $\S 6.1$ benchmark.

\subsection{Single-seed result: features lift AUC and per-row chain recovery}
\label{sec:phase4-features-result}

We compare \code{--features none} (local-only fit, $d = n+E = 8{,}392$)
to \code{--features real} (local + global feature terms,
$d = 8{,}402$). On seed=42, $T=20$, $|X_o|=601$, $\code{maxiter}=1500$,
the headline numbers are summarised in \cref{tab:phase4-features}.

Two readings are worth separating. First, the $+0.032$ AUC lift sits
outside the previously characterised $\pm 0.024$ single-seed SUMO-instance
noise band (iter-15 of the project), so the directional change is
robust to the dominant noise source. Second --- and this is the more
methodologically important reading --- the stacked Pearson on
\texttt{[all branching]} rises from $+0.132$ to $+0.215$, and the
per-row cosine median from $+0.095$ to $+0.435$. Per the iter-18
diagnostic, those are the metrics that distinguish ``the fit improved
because the chains got more accurate per-row'' from ``the fit improved
because the chains drifted in helpful opposite directions in aggregate''.
Both moved positively, supporting the stronger chain-recovery claim.

The one metric that barely moves is the fraction of rows with positive
cosine (only $+1.9$ pp on \texttt{[all branching]}, $58.6\%$ at
\texttt{[X\_o branching]}). Features sharpen the contrast magnitude in
the rows that were already approximately right, but do not flip the
sign on the $\sim 45\%$ of branching states whose fitted contrast points
the wrong way. This suggests the residual misspecification is
structural to the $1$-step softmax family --- a hypothesis we test next.

\subsection{Higher-order memory diagnostic: refuting the 2-step parametric extension}
\label{sec:phase4-2step}

A natural hypothesis for the still-flipped rows is that the
$1$-step Markov assumption is empirically violated: real drivers'
next-edge choice depends on \emph{both} the current and previous edge
(``do not U-turn'', ``keep on the arterial''), and the $1$-step softmax
cannot encode that dependence. If true, no fitting effort in the
$1$-step family can do better than the $1$-step empirical detector.

To test this we run the diagnostic of \cref{sec:2step-diag} on the same
training trajectories that produced the SUMO empirical chains. The
$2$-step empirical detector \emph{underperforms} the $1$-step one
(\cref{tab:phase4-2step}).

The $2$-step detector \emph{loses} $\sim 0.02$ AUC despite using a
strictly richer conditional, with the loss attributable to the data
sparsity (the empirical $\widehat{p}_{2}$ is Laplace-smoothed but
the $(x_{t-1}, x_t)$ pair space is $|X|^2 \approx 5.8\text{M}$ at the
bigger-bbox scale, and most of it is empty). The headline conclusion
is that \textbf{the $0.685$ ceiling at $T=20$ on this dataset
is the $1$-step Markov data ceiling}: there is no higher-order memory
left to access. A parametric $2$-step inverter would not help. This
closes a non-trivial future-work item (\cref{sec:methodology}: ``the
parametric $2$-step extension'') as not worth building.

\subsection{Trajectory-length sweep: lifting the ceiling and the detector together}
\label{sec:phase4-tsweep}

The $0.685$ ceiling is specific to the chosen test trajectory length
$T = 20$. The log-likelihood-ratio score $\Lam(\tau)$ is a sum of $T$
independent log-ratio increments, so its variance scales as $\sim T$.
Longer trajectories produce a tighter score distribution between the
two regimes and a higher achievable AUC; the data is ``the same'',
only the observation window is wider.

\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.78\linewidth]{fig02_phase4_tsweep.png}
\caption{Tier~2 $T$-sweep on SUMO bigger-bbox, seed~$42$,
\code{features=real}. The empirical $1$-step Markov ceiling

Two findings worth recording. First, the empirical ceiling rises from
$0.685$ to $0.801$ between $T=20$ and $T=40$, matching an earlier
small-bbox observation (iter-13 of the project) and confirming that
the ceiling at $T=20$ was an artefact of the truncation window, not a
fundamental data limit. Second, the per-row Pearson is constant across
$T$ at $\sim +0.21$. $T$ is purely a scoring-window lever: it lets the
trajectory-level score discriminate more sharply, but it does not
change the chain that was fit. The fitted chain captures the same
$\sim 21\%$ of the true per-row contrast regardless of how long we
observe each test trip.

The strongest single-seed SUMO headline is therefore $\AUC = 0.708$
at $T=40$, $69\%$ of the gap closed against the verified $0.801$
ceiling, with a stable per-row chain Pearson of $+0.216$ on
\texttt{[all branching]}.

\subsection{The $f+g$ extension at scale: documented infeasibility}
\label{sec:phase4-fg-infeasible}

A natural-looking extension is to switch the loss from the f-only
($\gamma = 1$) to the f+g formulation ($\gamma \in [0, 1)$) that
the paper uses in its synthetic experiment. Three sweep attempts were
made at \code{maxiter} $\in \{1500, 300, 100\}$ and $|X_o| \in
\{601, 120, 72\}$. None completed in finite wall-clock; representative
behaviour is that the rush-chain fit at $\code{maxiter}=1500,
|X_o|=601$ ran $9.5$ hours without producing an L-BFGS termination
signal.

Diagnostic: the empirical hitting-rate matrix $\gobs \in
\mathbb{R}^{|X_o| \times |X_o|}$ has $84$--$89\%$ of cells at the
$10^{-3}$ floor (no observed transitions between the corresponding
station pairs). Each L-BFGS iteration costs
$O(|X_o| \cdot n^3)$ via the per-state LU decomposition needed for
the hitting-rate gradient, and the resulting loss landscape is
severely under-constrained (effective informative cells $\sim 600$
against $d = 8{,}402$ parameters). L-BFGS line-search backtracks
indefinitely. A Beta-Binomial shrinkage of the floor (designed to
mitigate the floor problem) does not change the compute cost.

This is documented as a real limitation, not a bug: the framework's
hitting-rate extension is computationally infeasible at this scale
under the L-BFGS optimiser without one of the deeper algorithmic
fixes the paper itself identifies as future work --- natural-gradient
descent (paper $\S 4.1$) or a sparse-$g$ formulation that skips the
empty cells. We do not pursue either in this thesis. The headline
remains the $f$-only detector with features.
