\section{Methodology}\label{sec:methodology}

This section specifies the partial-observation routing-influence filter
end-to-end. \cref{sec:filter-design} states the filter as a designed
system --- its objective, inputs, estimator, scoring rule, decision
output, diagnostic audits, and failure checks --- and gives the
pipeline schematic (\cref{fig:filter-schematic}) and algorithmic
specification (Algorithm~\ref{alg:filter}).
\cref{sec:morimura-formulation,sec:loss-gradients} then specify the
inverse-Markov-chain estimator that sits inside the filter, in
the notation used throughout the rest of the dissertation.
\cref{sec:features}--\ref{sec:od-rebal} document the three filter
extensions developed in this work (feature-regularised variant,
$2$-step memory audit, OD-rebalancing pre-step), and
\cref{sec:detector,sec:design-rationale} cover the scoring rule, the
per-row diagnostic, and the design rationale that ties each component
to the failure mode it addresses.

\subsection{Filter design overview}
\label{sec:filter-design}

The filter is the designed artefact of this dissertation; the
inverse-Markov-chain method of Morimura et al.~\cite{morimura2013} is one component
inside it. The filter is specified as a closed system before fixing any
notation, so that the experimental tiers in
\cref{sec:phase1-synthetic,sec:phase4-sumo,sec:phase5-xuancheng} can be
read as validations of individual design choices rather than as a
sequence of reproduced results.

\paragraph{Objective.} Given two routing regimes $A$ and $B$ over the
same road network (e.g.\ unguided vs.\ sat-navigation-rerouted, or
off-peak vs.\ rush-hour), decide for each test trajectory $\tau$ whether
it was generated by regime $A$ or regime $B$, using only partial
network observations at a sparse subset of stations $\Xo$.

\paragraph{Inputs.} A directed road graph $\mathcal{X}$ (largest
strongly-connected, passenger-allowed component); a subset of observed
states $\Xo \subseteq \mathcal{X}$; stationary observations $\fobs_A,
\fobs_B$ on $\Xo$ (regime-labelled flow counts); optional hitting-rate
observations $\gobs_A, \gobs_B$ on $\Xo \times \Xo$; test trajectories
$\{\tau_k\}$; a decision threshold $\eta$.

\paragraph{Estimator.} For each regime $r \in \{A, B\}$, fit a
softmax-parametric Markov chain (\cref{sec:morimura-formulation}) by
minimising the regularised log-ratio loss of \cite{morimura2013}
(\cref{sec:loss-gradients}). The estimator is FD-verified
(\cref{app:regularisation-fd-primer}, \cref{sec:phase1-synthetic}) and validated against the original
synthetic benchmark before being deployed inside the filter.

\paragraph{Scoring rule.} For each test trajectory $\tau$, compute the
per-step log-likelihood-ratio score $\Lam(\tau) = \sum_t [\log
\hat{p}_B(x_{t+1}\!\mid\!x_t) - \log \hat{p}_A(x_{t+1}\!\mid\!x_t)]$
under the two fitted kernels.

\paragraph{Decision output.} Mark $\tau$ as
externally-influenced / regime-$B$-like if $\Lam(\tau) \ge \eta$, else
as intrinsic / regime-$A$-like. The threshold $\eta$ is a deployment
choice; validation reports the AUC over $\eta$.

\paragraph{Diagnostic audits.} Alongside the scalar AUC the filter
emits: (i) the per-row Pearson and per-state cosine of the fitted
regime contrast against a full-observation empirical reference at
branching states (\cref{sec:detector}); (ii) an empirical $2$-step
memory audit on the same trajectories (\cref{sec:2step-diag}); (iii) an
OD-rebalancing report when real-world regimes have different population
mixes (\cref{sec:od-rebal}); (iv) a multistart basin check on the
estimator's optimum (\cref{sec:phase4-multistart,sec:phase5-multistart}).
These audits judge whether the AUC should be trusted before any
headline score is interpreted.

\paragraph{Failure and confound checks.} The filter is reported as
unreliable when (a) the per-row Pearson is near zero while the AUC is
high (aggregate-bias-driven detection); (b) regimes have a different
OD mix that the rebalancer cannot fully control at the chosen
granularity $K$; (c) the multistart check finds an optimum better than
the chosen one; (d) the empirical $\gobs$ matrix is floor-dominated and
the $f+g$ extension stops being tractable (\cref{sec:phase4-fg-infeasible}).
Each of these is documented in the validation tiers as a failure mode
that the design must declare honestly rather than mask.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Partial-observation routing-influence filter}
\label{alg:filter}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Road graph $\mathcal{X}$, observed subset $\Xo \subseteq \mathcal{X}$,
  regime stationary observations $\fobs_A, \fobs_B$ on $\Xo$,
  optional hitting-rate observations $\gobs_A, \gobs_B$ on $\Xo \times \Xo$,
  test trajectories $\{\tau_k\}$, decision threshold $\eta$
\Ensure Filter decisions $\{d_k\}$, trajectory scores $\{\Lam(\tau_k)\}$, trust diagnostics
\State $\hat{P}_A \gets \textsc{InverseMC-Fit}(\mathcal{X}, \Xo, \fobs_A, \gobs_A)$ \Comment{regime-$A$ kernel}
\State $\hat{P}_B \gets \textsc{InverseMC-Fit}(\mathcal{X}, \Xo, \fobs_B, \gobs_B)$ \Comment{regime-$B$ kernel}
\For{each test trajectory $\tau_k$}
  \State $\Lam(\tau_k) \gets \sum_t \bigl[\log \hat{p}_B(x_{t+1}\!\mid\!x_t) - \log \hat{p}_A(x_{t+1}\!\mid\!x_t)\bigr]$
  \State $d_k \gets \mathbb{1}[\Lam(\tau_k) \ge \eta]$ \Comment{$1$ = externally-influenced / regime-$B$-like}
\EndFor
\State \textbf{Audit:} compute branching-state per-row Pearson and per-state cosine of
  $\Delta_{\mathrm{fit}} = \hat{P}_B - \hat{P}_A$ vs.\ a full-observation empirical
  reference $\Delta_{\mathrm{emp}}$
\State \textbf{Audit:} compare $\Lam$ against an empirical $2$-step detector built
  from the same trajectories (memory-order check)
\State \textbf{Audit:} if regimes are real-world populations, rebalance to a
  common $(\mathrm{zone}_o, \mathrm{zone}_d)$ distribution and report sensitivity to $K$
\State \textbf{Audit:} multistart \textsc{InverseMC-Fit} from $K_{\mathrm{ms}}$
  random inits and confirm the chosen optimum is the validation-best
\State \Return $\{d_k\}, \{\Lam(\tau_k)\}$, audit diagnostics
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Parametric chain and observation model}
\label{sec:morimura-formulation}

The state space $\mathcal{X}$ is the set of directed road edges in the
largest strongly-connected component of the network. For each state
$x \in \mathcal{X}$, the next-state distribution is the softmax of
\cref{eq:softmax} over the legal successors $\mathrm{adj}(x)$, with the
score
\begin{equation}
s(x, x') \;=\; \omega^{\mathrm{loc}}_{(x,x')}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\phi_T(x')^\top \omega^{\mathrm{glo}_1}}_{\text{destination-feature term}}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\psi(x, x')^\top \omega^{\mathrm{glo}_2}}_{\text{edge-feature term}}.
\label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
Without the global terms ($d_T = d_\psi = 0$) the model has one
parameter per directed edge; with global terms it adds $d_T + d_\psi$
shared parameters that apply across all edges of compatible type.
The initial distribution $\pI$ is similarly parametrised by per-state
weights $\nu \in \mathbb{R}^n$ via $\pI(x) \propto \exp(\nu_x)$.

The absorbing-corrected stationary mixture is defined as
\begin{equation}
P \;=\; \beta\,\Pchain \;+\; (1-\beta)\,\bm{1}\pI^{\!\top},
\qquad
\pi^{\!\top} P = \pi^{\!\top}, \quad \bm{1}^{\!\top}\pi = 1,
\label{eq:stationary}
\end{equation}
which models the long-run occupancy of a chain that occasionally restarts
from $\pI$ with probability $1-\beta$ per step. The hitting-rate matrix
$H(\Pchain) \in [0,1]^{|\Xo|\times|\Xo|}$ has entries equal to the
$\beta$-discounted first-passage probability between observation states,
obtained as $H_{ij} = [(I - \beta\Pchain^{(\setminus j)})^{-1}]_{ix_j}$
where $\Pchain^{(\setminus j)}$ zeroes row $j$ to encode absorption.
The Morimura observation model says that noisy $\fobs(x)$ is observed for
$x \in \Xo$ and noisy $\gobs(x, x')$ for $(x, x') \in \Xo \times \Xo$,
and the model's $\pi$ and $H$ are matched to these observations.
This is a stationarity approximation. The theory treats $\fobs$ as a
sample from the chain's stationary distribution and $\gobs$ as stationary
hitting-rate evidence, whereas the SUMO and Xuancheng experiments observe
finite time windows with time-varying congestion and regime composition.
Throughout the dissertation the fitted chain should therefore be read as
a time-aggregated Markov surrogate for the observed window rather than
literal behavioural ground truth. The empirical full-observation transition
matrix used in the diagnostics is likewise a finite-data reference rather
than an unattainable ``true'' chain.

\subsection{Regularised loss and analytic gradients}
\label{sec:loss-gradients}

Following \cite{morimura2013} Eqs.~7--9, the loss is the sum of a
stationary-match term, a hitting-rate-match term, and a Tikhonov ridge:
\begin{align}
L(\theta) &= \gamma\,L_d(\theta) + (1-\gamma)\,L_h(\theta) + \lambda\,R(\theta), \\
L_d(\theta) &= \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i, j \in \Xo}\bigl(\log\tfrac{\pi_i}{\pi_j} - \log\tfrac{f_i}{f_j}\bigr)^2, \\
L_h(\theta) &= \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i, j \in \Xo}\bigl(\log H_{ij} - \log g_{ij}\bigr)^2, \\
R(\theta) &= \tfrac{1}{2}\|\theta\|^2.
\end{align}
Both data terms are log-ratio losses, which is what makes them
optimisable without normalising constants on the observations
\cite{morimura2013}: any multiplicative scale on $\fobs$ or $\gobs$
cancels in the difference. The gradients are derived using the
identity for the derivative of $\log\pi$ under a softmax-parametric
chain (paper Eq.~12) and the derivative of $\log H_{ij}$ under an
absorbing-chain solve (Eq.~15). The paper's local-only gradients
$\partial L / \partial \nu$ and $\partial L / \partial \omega^{\mathrm{loc}}$
are reproduced unchanged here; the omega-global
gradients (paper Eqs.~17--18 state the parametric extension but
do not derive the gradients) are obtained by writing the chain-rule contribution of
$\omega^{\mathrm{glo}_1}$ and $\omega^{\mathrm{glo}_2}$ through the
score \eqref{eq:score} and chaining through $\nabla_\theta \log \pi$ and
$\nabla_\theta \log H$.

The analytic gradients are verified by centred finite differences
(\code{fd\_check\_features.py}) at random parameter draws across
all four blocks ($\nu$, $\omega^{\mathrm{loc}}$, $\omega^{\mathrm{glo}_1}$,
$\omega^{\mathrm{glo}_2}$). The check uses a noise-aware tolerance
($\code{rtol}=10^{-3}$ relative or $\code{atol}=5 \times 10^{-2}$
absolute, an \code{np.allclose}-style criterion) to handle the
machine-precision FD round-off floor that dominates near-zero gradient
components. The Ingolstadt SUMO graph passes at machine precision
($\sim 10^{-8}$ relative error) on the clearly non-zero components.

\subsection{Extension 1: real edge features from the network topology}
\label{sec:features}

The Morimura framework treats $\phi_T$ and $\psi$ as user-supplied global
features. In simulated $\S 6.1$ both are drawn iid
$\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ in their setup; in their real-world Nairobi case
they use network attributes (road class, segment length, number of
lanes) extracted from the underlying OSM data.

The latter convention is followed here. From the SUMO network's
\code{.net.xml}, the following per-edge attributes are extracted and
assembled into $\phi_T$ and $\psi$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Destination-state features $\phi_T(x') \in \mathbb{R}^{8}$.}
For each destination edge $x'$: a $6$-dimensional one-hot road-class
indicator (motorway / primary / tertiary / residential / service / other),
plus the standardised log of the speed limit and the standardised log of
the lane count.
\item \textbf{Directed-edge features $\psi(x, x') \in \mathbb{R}^{2}$.}
For each directed edge $(x, x')$: the cosine of the turn angle at the
intersection (computed from the polyline geometries of $x$ and $x'$),
and the standardised log ratio of the destination speed limit to the
source speed limit. Both components are standardised to zero mean and
unit variance across the edge set.
\end{itemize}
With these definitions $d_T + d_\psi = 10$, so feature inclusion adds
just $10$ shared parameters across the entire chain. The
expectation is that this acts as a strong regularising prior: edges of
the same road class share a common preference component, even if their
local $\omega^{\mathrm{loc}}_{(x, x')}$ weights are estimated noisily.

\subsection{Extension 2: empirical 2-step Markov diagnostic}
\label{sec:2step-diag}

The $1$-step softmax chain in \eqref{eq:softmax} embeds the standard
Markov assumption that the next edge depends only on the current edge.
A natural concern is that this assumption is empirically violated:
real drivers' next-edge choice may depend on both the current
edge and the previous edge (e.g.\ avoiding a U-turn, preserving heading,
continuing on a high-priority arterial). If so, no fitting effort under
the $1$-step model class can close the gap to the full-observation
empirical $1$-step reference.

This is tested by constructing an empirical $2$-step detector with no
fitting. From the training trajectories of each regime, the procedure counts
$(x_{t-1}, x_t, x_{t+1})$ triples and forms the conditional probability
$\widehat{p}_{2}(x_{t+1} \mid x_t, x_{t-1})$ as the Laplace-smoothed
empirical ratio. The detector then scores a test trajectory $\tau$ as
\[
\Lam_{2}(\tau) \;=\; \sum_{t}\bigl[\log\widehat{p}_{2}^{(B)}(\tau_{t+1}\mid\tau_t,\tau_{t-1}) - \log\widehat{p}_{2}^{(A)}(\tau_{t+1}\mid\tau_t,\tau_{t-1})\bigr],
\]
falling back to the $1$-step empirical conditional on the first step or
any unseen $(x_{t-1}, x_t)$ context. The AUC of $\Lam_{2}$ vs.\ the
$1$-step empirical $\Lam_{1}$ indicates whether higher-order memory
exists in the data. Crucially, the $2$-step detector is built from the
same trajectories and uses the same training/test split, so any
AUC difference is attributable to memory order alone.

\subsection{Extension 3: OD-rebalancing for real-world regime contrast}
\label{sec:od-rebal}

In controlled simulation, the intrinsic and sat-navigation regimes
share the same OD pairs by construction (\cref{sec:sumo}): the only
difference is the routing rule. In real-world AVI data this control is
not available: rush-hour drivers and off-peak drivers are
different populations with different origin/destination
distributions, and any chain difference between them reflects both
their different OD mix and their different routing-given-OD choices. The
former is uninteresting (commuters vs.\ discretionary trips have
different origins by definition); the latter is what the framework is
designed to detect.

To control for OD mix, the real-world loader introduces an OD-rebalancing
step. The procedure is:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Cluster the network's junctions into $K$ spatial zones via
$k$-means on their $(x, y)$ coordinates (standardised). Each edge is
labelled by its from-zone and its to-zone.
\item For each trip, compute its origin zone (from-zone of the first
edge in its trajectory) and its destination zone (to-zone of the last
edge).
\item Bin all regime-A trips and all regime-B trips by $(\text{zone}_o, \text{zone}_d)$.
\item For each shared OD cell, take $\min(n^A_{od}, n^B_{od})$ trips
from each regime (sampled without replacement). The matched pools
have identical marginal $(\text{zone}_o, \text{zone}_d)$ distribution.
\end{enumerate}
Any chain contrast measured on the rebalanced pools is then
attributable to within-OD routing-choice differences after OD-mix shift has
been removed as an explanation. The granularity $K$ is a tuning parameter:
smaller $K$
gives larger per-cell counts but coarser matching (and can
over-correct, washing out within-OD signal); larger $K$
gives finer matching but smaller per-cell counts. Because the result is
sensitive to this choice, a full sweep $K \in \{8, 16, 32, 64\}$ is
reported in \cref{sec:phase5-reading} rather than relying on a single
default.

\subsection{Filter scoring rule and diagnostic audits}
\label{sec:detector}

Once two chains $\Pchain^{(A)}, \Pchain^{(B)}$ are fitted, the
per-trajectory log-likelihood-ratio detector scores
\[
\Lam(\tau) \;=\; \sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\bigl[\log\pT^{(B)}(\tau_{t+1}\mid\tau_t) - \log\pT^{(A)}(\tau_{t+1}\mid\tau_t)\bigr],
\]
and the AUC of $\Lam$ against the labelled test trajectories
($N$ per regime, $T$-edge windows) summarises detection performance.
Because AUC alone can reflect aggregate chain-drift rather than per-row
chain accuracy, the filter additionally reports
a contrast-correlation diagnostic at branching states (states
with out-degree $> 1$): for each such state $x$, the diagnostic computes
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{\mathrm{fit}}(x, \cdot) &= p^{(B)}_{T,\mathrm{fit}}(x, \cdot) - p^{(A)}_{T,\mathrm{fit}}(x, \cdot), \\
\Delta_{\mathrm{emp}}(x, \cdot) &= p^{(B)}_{T,\mathrm{emp}}(x, \cdot) - p^{(A)}_{T,\mathrm{emp}}(x, \cdot),
\end{aligned}
\]
where the empirical chain $P_{T,\mathrm{emp}}$ is the
full-observation Laplace-smoothed transition matrix from the trajectories.
The stacked Pearson correlation of $\Delta_{\mathrm{fit}}$
against $\Delta_{\mathrm{emp}}$ over all branching-state legal-edge entries
is reported alongside the per-state cosine similarity distribution
(median, mean, and
fraction of states with positive cosine). A high AUC with low Pearson
indicates aggregate-bias-driven detection; a high AUC and a
non-trivial Pearson together support the stronger ``chain recovery''
claim.

\paragraph{Validation split.} In the SUMO and Xuancheng scoring scripts,
the trajectory-level test set is held out from the empirical transition
matrix used as $\Delta_{\mathrm{emp}}$ and from the empirical-reference
LLR scorer. The inverse-MC fit, however, is trained from aggregate station
counts $\fobs$ for the whole regime window; those counts are not recomputed
after removing the held-out vehicles. This mirrors the intended deployment
setting: sparse counters are available as period aggregates, whereas
labelled trajectories are only used for validation. In consequence, the
reported AUCs are held out at the trajectory-scoring level rather
than as fully out-of-sample sensor-count experiments.

\subsection{Design rationale: each extension targets a specific failure mode}
\label{sec:design-rationale}

The extensions above form a deliberate sequence, with each step addressing
a failure mode exposed by the previous one:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Real edge features} (\cref{sec:features}) address sparse
observation and parameter explosion: $10$ shared parameters let edges of
the same road class borrow strength, regularising the per-edge weights
that are otherwise estimated from few or no observed transitions.
\item \textbf{Per-row Pearson / cosine diagnostics} (\cref{sec:detector})
address the risk that AUC improves for aggregate-bias reasons rather than
correct chain recovery, by measuring the fitted-vs-empirical contrast
directly at branching states.
\item \textbf{The empirical $2$-step diagnostic} (\cref{sec:2step-diag})
tests whether higher-order memory is an obvious missing lever before any
parametric $2$-step model is built.
\item \textbf{OD-rebalancing} (\cref{sec:od-rebal}) addresses the
real-world population-mix confound that has no analogue in the controlled
SUMO comparison.
\item \textbf{Multistart / basin checks} (\cref{app:lbfgs-primer},
\cref{sec:phase4-multistart},
\cref{sec:phase5-multistart}) address the possibility that a result is an
L-BFGS optimiser artefact rather than a property of the data.
\end{itemize}
Where AUCs lie close to chance, the honest interpretation also requires
quantifying sampling noise. Tier~3 therefore reports stratified
vehicle-level bootstrap confidence intervals
(\cref{app:bootstrap-primer}, \cref{sec:phase5-reading},
\cref{sec:disc-limits}): after the chains,
station set $\Xo$, OD matching, and train/test split are fixed, test
trajectories are resampled with replacement within each regime and the
AUC is recomputed. These CIs capture test-trajectory sampling noise only.
They do not include variation from refitting the chains, resampling
observation stations, changing the OD cells, or redrawing the validation
split. The remaining open statistical extensions are therefore an
OD-cell bootstrap and an $X_o$-selection sensitivity sweep, both of which
require full refits rather than cheap score resampling.
