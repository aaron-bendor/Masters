\begin{abstract}
\noindent Urban traffic increasingly carries the fingerprint of external routing
influence: satellite-navigation systems and live traffic services reroute their
users around congestion, producing observed traffic patterns that no longer
reflect drivers' intrinsic route preferences. Recovering those intrinsic
preferences from partial network observations is the inverse problem of
\citeauthor{morimura2013}~\cite{morimura2013}, who introduced a softmax-parametric Markov-chain
inverter that fits a transition kernel to observed stationary
distributions and hitting rates at a small subset of observation states.
We extend that framework with three contributions that target real-world
applicability. First, we wire SUMO-derived edge attributes (road class,
speed, lanes, turn geometry) into the inverter as global features and
show, across a five-seed SUMO sweep, that they robustly improve
\emph{per-row chain recovery} --- the stacked Pearson correlation
between fitted and empirical regime contrasts rises on all five seeds
(mean $+0.050 \pm 0.023$, paired $p \approx 0.008$) --- whereas the
aggregate detection-AUC lift is seed-dependent and not statistically
robust ($+0.024 \pm 0.026$, reversing on one of five seeds). On the
representative seed the detector reaches $\AUC = 0.708$ at T=40 against
an empirically verified $1$-step Markov data ceiling of $0.801$. Second, we settle whether a parametric $2$-step extension would
close the remaining gap by comparing a $1$-step empirical detector to a
$2$-step empirical detector built from the same trajectories: the $2$-step
\emph{underperforms}, refuting higher-order memory as the missing lever
and identifying the chosen $1$-step softmax family as the binding model-class
constraint. Third, we port the entire pipeline to a real-world urban
Automatic-Vehicle-Identification dataset from Xuancheng, China
\cite{ma2026xuancheng} ($\sim 10.5\text{M}$ reconstructed vehicle trips
over April~2023), with an OD-rebalancing extension that controls
for the population-mix confound absent in simulated data. The OD-rebalancing
reveals that approximately three-quarters of the apparent rush-vs-off-peak
chain difference in raw pooled data is attributable to OD-mix between
commuter and discretionary trip populations, not to routing-choice
differences: the empirical $1$-step Markov ceiling falls from
$\AUC = 0.573$ on the un-matched pool to $\AUC = 0.518$ on the OD-matched
pool. After controlling for this confound, the within-OD routing
contrast is only $0.018$ above chance --- below the detectability
threshold of the framework under the chosen behavioural proxy. Across all three validation tiers,
methodology and pipeline remain identical; only the data changes.
The resulting work establishes both an
upper bound (synthetic reproduction within $\sim 0.05$ RMAE of the
original paper) and an honest lower bound on what 1-step Markov inverse
methods can achieve on real urban traffic without ground-truth rerouting
labels.
\end{abstract}
