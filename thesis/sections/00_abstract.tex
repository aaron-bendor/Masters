\begin{abstract}
\noindent Satellite-navigation systems and live traffic services reroute their
users around congestion, so observed urban traffic increasingly reflects a
mixture of drivers' intrinsic route preferences and externally-influenced
routing. Recovering a route-choice transition kernel from partial network
observations is the inverse problem of Morimura, Osogami and
Id\'e~\cite{morimura2013}, who introduced a softmax-parametric Markov-chain
inverter that fits the kernel to observed stationary distributions and
hitting rates at a small subset of observation states. We adapt that framework
into a two-regime \emph{detection} pipeline and evaluate it across three
tiers: a synthetic reproduction of the original benchmark, a controlled SUMO
simulation comparing static unguided routing against sat-navigation
rerouting, and a real-world Automatic-Vehicle-Identification (AVI) dataset
from Xuancheng, China~\cite{ma2026xuancheng} ($\sim 10.5$M reconstructed
vehicle trips over April~2023) using rush-hour versus off-peak travel as a
behavioural proxy. Three findings stand out. First, wiring SUMO-derived edge
attributes (road class, speed, lanes, turn geometry) into the inverter as
global features robustly improves \emph{per-row chain recovery} across a
five-seed sweep --- the stacked Pearson correlation between fitted and
full-observation empirical regime contrasts rises on all five seeds (mean
$+0.050 \pm 0.023$, paired $p \approx 0.008$) --- whereas the aggregate
detection-AUC lift is seed-dependent and not statistically robust
($+0.024 \pm 0.026$, reversing on one of five seeds). Second, an empirical
$2$-step detector built from the same trajectories does not improve over the
$1$-step empirical reference ($0.666 < 0.685$), which weakens the case for a
parametric $2$-step extension as the immediate next lever, though it does not
rule out richer destination-aware or hierarchical higher-order models. Third,
on the real AVI data --- where no ground-truth sat-navigation labels exist ---
an OD-rebalancing step shows that roughly three-quarters of the apparent
rush-versus-off-peak chain difference in raw pooled data is attributable to
OD-mix between commuter and discretionary trip populations rather than to
routing choice: the full-observation empirical $1$-step reference AUC falls
from $0.573$ on the un-matched pool to $0.518$ on the OD-matched pool, leaving
a within-OD routing contrast only $0.018$ above chance --- not detectable
under this proxy, model class, and data split. The same inverse-MC scoring
core is reused across tiers, while the loader, regime definition, and
OD-control logic differ by tier. The contribution is therefore both
constructive and limiting: the method works as a regime detector under
controlled labelled simulation, but real-world sat-navigation detection
remains constrained by the absence of labels, by OD-mix confounding, and by
the $1$-step model class. The work establishes both the potential and the
limits of $1$-step inverse-Markov-chain methods under partial observation.
\end{abstract}
