\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{Context: urban traffic and the external-influence problem}
\label{sec:context}

The widespread adoption of satellite-navigation applications such as Google
Maps, Waze, and TomTom has structurally altered how vehicles use urban road
networks. These systems no longer merely report a single shortest path:
they reroute users in real time around observed congestion, redistributing
traffic across alternative arteries on time-scales of minutes to hours.
The consequence is that any traffic observation aggregating across modern
drivers reflects a \emph{mixture} of two distinct routing rules: the
intrinsic preferences a driver would express in the absence of guidance
(habit, scenic preference, fuel-cost optimisation, familiarity), and the
external recommendations of a sat-navigation system reacting to live
network state.

This mixture creates a problem for downstream traffic models. Demand
estimation, route-choice inference, transport-policy evaluation, and
infrastructure-investment decisions all rely on revealed-preference data
that is assumed to encode \emph{driver} preferences. When a sizeable
fraction of observed trips are guided by an external recommender that
itself responds to congestion (and thereby to other guided trips), the
revealed data encodes a partially synthetic, congestion-coupled signal
that the underlying driver population would not have produced unaided.
Identifying and filtering this externally-influenced traffic, or at minimum
quantifying its presence, has therefore become a methodological
prerequisite for any inference about driver behaviour in modern urban
environments.

\subsection{This work: a partial-observation routing-influence filter}
\label{sec:intro-filter}

The central artefact of this dissertation is a designed pipeline ---
called here the \emph{partial-observation routing-influence filter} ---
that ingests a road graph, a small subset of observation stations $\Xo$
(loop detectors, AVI gantries, or simulated counts), and two
regime-labelled flow snapshots (e.g.\ rerouting on / rerouting off,
rush-hour / off-peak), fits one inverse-Markov-chain model per regime,
and emits a per-trajectory log-likelihood-ratio score together with a
suite of trust diagnostics. The pipeline is the deliverable; the
inverse-Markov-chain estimator is the component inside it, and the
three experimental tiers reported in this dissertation exist to justify,
parameterise, and stress-test the filter's design choices.

A long line of work models route choice as a Markov chain over road
network edges, in which each driver, conditional on their current edge,
selects a next edge with probabilities determined by local and global
features of the network. \citet{morimura2013} introduced an
\emph{inverse} formulation that fits the parameters of such a chain
from only partial observations of the network --- specifically, from
vehicle counts at a small subset $\Xo$ and optionally from pairwise
hitting rates between those stations. Their softmax-parametric chain
admits an analytic gradient of the regularised log-ratio loss, enabling
reliable fitting on networks of several thousand edges with
$20\text{--}90\%$ observation coverage. This is the estimator we adopt
inside the filter. The original method is presented as a
\emph{single-chain recovery} procedure and was validated by its authors
on synthetic data and one Nairobi case study; turning it into a
trajectory-level \emph{detector and filter} for one routing regime against
another, audited for the failure modes that show up only at urban scale,
is the work this dissertation does on top of it.

\subsection{Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

The contributions of this work are design-led. Each item below is a
component of the filter, a design decision audited by experiment, or a
diagnostic that establishes when the filter can or cannot be trusted.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A partial-observation filter for externally-influenced
routing.} A pipeline that maps a road graph plus regime-labelled partial
observations to a per-trajectory log-likelihood-ratio score and a filter
decision. The architecture is specified end-to-end in
\cref{sec:filter-design} (objective, inputs, estimator, scoring rule,
decision output, diagnostic audits) and codified as Algorithm~\ref{alg:filter},
with a schematic in \cref{fig:filter-schematic}.

\item \textbf{An inverse-MC estimation engine validated through Morimura
reproduction.} A faithful, FD-verified re-implementation of the
softmax-parametric inverter --- including the omega-global gradients the
paper states but does not derive in full --- whose recovery curve
reproduces $\S 6.1$ of \citet{morimura2013} closely for observation
fractions $\Xo/n \ge 0.10$. Tier~1 (\cref{sec:phase1-synthetic})
validates the estimator that sits inside the filter, not the filter as a
whole.

\item \textbf{A feature-regularised filter variant using road-network
attributes.} A version of the filter in which the estimator's global
score terms ingest real per-edge attributes from the SUMO network's
\code{.net.xml} (road class, speed limit, lane count, turn angle, speed
ratio): $10$ shared parameters that let edges of the same class borrow
strength across the chain. Across a five-seed SUMO sweep this variant
robustly improves per-row chain recovery (stacked Pearson
$+0.050 \pm 0.023$, $p \approx 0.008$, positive on all five seeds), while
its aggregate detection-AUC lift is seed-dependent
($+0.024 \pm 0.026$, reversing on one of five seeds) and not claimed as
robust.

\item \textbf{A trajectory log-likelihood-ratio scoring rule.} A
per-trip detection score $\Lam(\tau) = \sum_t [\log \hat
p_B(x_{t+1}\!\mid\!x_t) - \log \hat p_A(x_{t+1}\!\mid\!x_t)]$ derived
from the two fitted regime kernels, with a tunable decision threshold
$\eta$. On the representative SUMO seed the score reaches
$\AUC = 0.644$ at $T=20$ against a full-observation empirical $1$-step
reference of $0.685$, rising to $\AUC = 0.708$ at $T=40$ against a
reference of $0.801$ as the scoring window widens.

\item \textbf{Diagnostic audits separating per-row chain recovery from
aggregate-AUC effects.} A contrast-correlation diagnostic at branching
states (per-row Pearson and per-state cosine of $\Delta_{\mathrm{fit}}$
against $\Delta_{\mathrm{emp}}$) that tells the user whether a high
detection AUC reflects correct chain recovery or merely aggregate
bias. This is the most important trust signal the filter emits: it
flagged the seed-dependence of the AUC lift in (3) and motivates the
filter's recommended reporting protocol.

\item \textbf{Controlled SUMO validation of the full filter under
labelled rerouting.} A bigger-bbox SUMO scenario
($n = 2{,}405$ edges) in which an identical population of vehicles is
simulated twice, once with no sat-navigation rerouting (static unguided
routing) and once with full sat-navigation rerouting. This is the only
tier in which the filter's regime labels are ground-truth; it answers
whether the designed pipeline works end-to-end when the detection
problem is correctly posed.

\item \textbf{An empirical higher-order memory audit.} A fitting-free
$2$-step detector --- counting $(x_{t-1}, x_t)\to x_{t+1}$ triples on the
same trajectories --- used to test whether a parametric $2$-step
extension is the obvious next filter lever. It does \emph{not} improve
over the $1$-step empirical reference
($\AUC_{2\text{-step}} = 0.666 < \AUC_{1\text{-step}} = 0.685$), so a
parametric $2$-step extension is deprioritised; richer destination-aware
or hierarchical higher-order models remain open.

\item \textbf{A simulator decomposition of the rush-vs-off-peak
proxy.} A $\rho \times d$ grid in SUMO that varies sat-navigation
adoption $\rho$ and demand $d$ independently across five repeat
seeds, separating the two effects that the Tier~3 behavioural proxy
otherwise bundles. The decomposition attributes $\sim\!96\%$ of the
peak-vs-off-peak routing signal to sat-navigation adoption and a
pure-congestion contribution within noise, bounding the
congestion-rerouting confound from below in the controlled regime
(\cref{sec:phase4-rho-demand}).

\item \textbf{A real-world Xuancheng stress test under OD-mix
confounding.} The filter is ported to $\sim 10.5$ million reconstructed
vehicle trips over April~2023 \cite{ma2026xuancheng} with a time-of-day
behavioural proxy (rush-hour vs.\ off-peak) substituting for absent
sat-navigation labels, and an OD-rebalancing pre-step subsamples each
regime to share a common
$(\mathrm{origin\ zone},\mathrm{destination\ zone})$ distribution. The
fitted $1$-step filter does not recover the rush-vs-off-peak contrast at
any zone granularity tested ($K \in \{8, 16, 32, 64\}$, every bootstrap
CI straddling chance), even though the full-observation reference weakly
carries it --- a granularity-robust honest negative result on this
behavioural proxy (\cref{sec:phase5-xuancheng}).
\end{enumerate}

A single CLI runs all three tiers via a \code{--dataset \{sumo,xuancheng\}}
flag; the repository structure is documented in \cref{app:repo}.
